# Three direct questions to Jev (2026-10-22)

Asked word for word with `jev-1.14.0`. Jev answers typed questions about a
supplied state, so each answer depends on the facts it was given (below).
These are calibrated model judgments, not market research.

## 1. "Will the people who are in the Apple ecosystem use this sharing platform?"

Yes/no question. Answer: **0.29**. State: Drift as a free, account-free,
direct local-network transfer app with native apps for all platforms; people
with an iPhone plus a Mac and/or iPad who already have AirDrop.

## 2. "What can I do so that people 100% use this?"

Asked as a choice among eight concrete changes, in both option orders (same
winner both times), plus "would Apple-ecosystem people use it with this?" for
each change. Same state as question 1.

| Change | Chosen as the top lever | Would use it |
|---|---:|---:|
| Share sheet and Finder right-click, as easy as AirDrop | **0.91** | 0.55 |
| Huge transfers faster and more reliable than AirDrop, with resume | 0.01 | **0.60** |
| Send over the internet between different networks | 0.04 | 0.42 |
| Auto-sync a folder such as camera photos | 0.03 | 0.48 |
| Clipboard text and links between devices | 0.00 | 0.49 |
| Keep sending with the iPhone locked or the app closed | 0.00 | 0.48 |
| Free, open source, no account | 0.00 | 0.42 |
| Focus on what AirDrop cannot do (Windows, Android, Linux) | 0.01 | 0.41 |

## 3. "Should I build it or not in this tough market to stand out and all? Should I build it or not?"

Yes/no question. Answer: **0.32**. State: the same product description, plus
the market: AirDrop and Quick Share built in; LocalSend (free, open source, all
platforms), KDE Connect and PairDrop; and everyday use of cloud drives,
WeTransfer and messaging apps.

**Decision:** finish Drift as a Mac-only side project.
